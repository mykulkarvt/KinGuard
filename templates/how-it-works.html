KinGuard — How It Works

KinGuard helps protect an elderly relative from phone and message scams — with a family member in the loop. If something feels wrong, the elder taps one button, and you get alerted straight away with what to say to them.

It uses two phones

That is the whole idea, and it is worth understanding before you set anything up:

  • The elder’s phone shows a few very large buttons. There is no account and no password — they never log in, and never have to remember anything.
  • Your phone (the family member) receives the alerts. This is the one with the login.

You do the setup once, on your own phone, and then send the elder a private link. It takes about five minutes.

Setting it up

  1. Create your account Your phone

    Go to Create an account and sign up with your email address and a password. Only the family member needs an account.

  2. Fill in the Setup screen Your phone

    You will be asked for:

    • Language — Hindi, Telugu, Kannada, Tamil or English. The elder’s phone will be entirely in the language you pick.
    • The elder’s name and phone number — so you can call them with one tap when an alert arrives.
    • Your name and phone number — so they can call you.
    • The elder’s needs — see the table further down. This changes how their phone looks and behaves.

  3. Send the elder their link Your phone

    When you save, KinGuard gives you a private link. Send it to the elder however you normally message them.

    Keep this link private. Anyone who opens it becomes the linked elder phone for your account. If you ever need to, you can issue a fresh link from Setup, which immediately stops the old one working.

  4. They open the link once Elder’s phone

    That single tap links their phone for good. After that the app just opens — no login, no password, nothing to remember. Ask them to add it to their Home Screen so it is easy to find in a hurry.

What happens during a scam

On the elder’s phone Elder’s phone

They open KinGuard and see one large red button — “I am scared — call my family now” — and below it a short list of the situations that actually happen:

  • Police or CBI threatening to arrest them (the “digital arrest” scam)
  • Someone asking them to move money to a “safe account”
  • A familiar voice urgently asking for money
  • A link about KYC, a blocked account or a refund
  • A prize, lottery, or a promise to double their money

Whichever they tap, the phone tells them plainly that it is a scam, what to do right now — hang up, send no money, give nobody an OTP — and offers to call you.

On your phone Your phone

The alert appears within seconds, showing what they reported and when. You get:

  • Call them now — one tap, using their name
  • What to say to them — three specific lines for that exact scam, so you do not have to think of them while worried
  • Report to 1930 — India’s cybercrime helpline
  • They are safe — I have handled it — clears the alert

Getting alerts when the app is closed

Open your family screen and tap “Get alerts even when closed”, then allow notifications. Without this, alerts only appear while you have the app open.

On iPhone this button only appears once KinGuard has been added to the Home Screen — that is an Apple restriction, not a KinGuard one. Tap Share, then Add to Home Screen, and open KinGuard from that icon. Until then the alerts still show while the page is open.

Making it easier to use

On the Setup screen you can tell KinGuard what the elder finds difficult. Their phone then opens that way automatically — they do not have to change any settings themselves.

If they haveTheir phone will
Trouble hearingVibrate when an alert arrives
Trouble seeingUse large text and high contrast, and read everything aloud automatically
Shaky hands or a weak gripShow even larger buttons
Memory difficulty or confusionSwitch to simple mode — just one big button

You can pick more than one, and change them later at any time.

Installing it as an app

KinGuard works in the browser, but it is much easier to reach as an app icon — which matters when someone is being pressured on a call.

  • Android: Chrome offers to install it, or use the menu and choose Add to Home screen.
  • iPhone: in Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen. On iPhone this is also what enables alerts while the app is closed.

It works offline for the parts that matter, so the elder’s screen still opens on a weak connection. Sending an alert does need internet on both phones.

Privacy, in short

  • Both phone numbers are encrypted before they are stored.
  • KinGuard does not read messages, listen to calls, or track location. The elder chooses what to report, by tapping a button.
  • Nothing is shared with anyone else.
  • You can delete your account and everything in it from inside the app, at any time, with no residual copy kept.
